## Design Specificity Verdict

**Specific, but its specificity is load-bearing on hover and on dark. Strip both and an unrelated indie studio could ship this unchanged.**

**LLM assessment.** Where it genuinely earns its identity: the `--amber-accent` / `--amber-text` split with the logomark dot hard-coded *outside* the theming system is a real idea executed at the token level, and `--mark-neutral` (Cool Warm Fog) being deliberately not `--fog` makes the diamond read as an object rather than as text — invisible to almost every visitor, and exactly what separates a designed system from a themed one. The accent-outlined app-page container (1.5px, 24px radius, wrapping hero + screenshots + prose + footer links) is the strongest structural move on the site: it makes a product page read as one object, and it is not a composition seen elsewhere. `ConicOrb` disabling its own animation below `md` is the stated restraint ethic showing up as an engineering decision.

Where it drifts toward the generic AI-era SaaS landing page DESIGN.md explicitly bans — and it does, specifically in **light mode**: the light hero is a full-viewport pastel violet/cyan/blue gradient wash behind a centered mark, a serif headline and one line of subcopy. That is the archetype. In dark mode the aurora is nearly invisible and the Look-Through Rule holds; in light mode the bands run 0.35–0.45 alpha and the gradient is the loudest thing on screen. **The Perceptual Parity Rule and the Look-Through Rule are in direct conflict, and light mode is where parity wins and the brand loses.**

Second drift: the bento grid's distinguishing feature — the 3× hover jump DESIGN.md calls "the personality of the entire system" — is pointer-only. On mobile, where PRODUCT.md says most visitors arrive, the grid is five near-identical dark rectangles distinguished by a small mark and a badge color. The signature interaction does not exist for the majority of users.

Third: `"AI code reviews that don't suck."` is the one line on the site written in someone else's voice. Everything around it — "The ratio, perfected.", "Contacts, untangled.", "Small by design" — is precise and quiet. This is startup swagger, on the card a developer sees first.

## Overall Impression

This site is doing something genuinely rare: it is built as the proof of the claim it makes. Zero third-party requests, verified live, on every route. No analytics, no consent banner, no embeds. A competitor can copy the copy and cannot copy that.

And it says so nowhere a visitor will see it.

That is the single biggest opportunity here — the strongest asset is buried inside `/privacy`, while the homepage spends a full viewport on a floating mark and a scroll cue, then hands a stranger five cards, four of which say COMING SOON, with no sentence anywhere converting "mostly unbuilt" into "deliberately small."

The second thing to say plainly: the craft is real and the failures are narrow. Contrast holds at every small tier in both themes, the lightbox is a properly implemented dialog with focus return to the originating thumbnail, heading structure is clean, nothing overflows. What's broken is concentrated in the two places PRODUCT.md says matter most — a store-required privacy URL that 404s onto an unbranded stock error page, and a support form that reports success it cannot verify. Those are the front door and the only interactive flow, and both fail quietly.

## What's Working

**The site is the argument.** Confirmed live: zero non-localhost requests on every route, fonts self-hosted at build time, no analytics, no embeds, no consent banner. The privacy claim isn't asserted, it's demonstrated. This is unfakeable and it is the most valuable thing about the site.

**Overlay semantics are unusually correct.** The lightbox is a real `dialog` with `aria-modal`, focus moved to the close button, Tab trapped, Escape closing, and focus returned to *the exact thumbnail that opened it* — all four verified live. The nav menus are honestly modeled as disclosures rather than falsely claiming `aria-modal`, because the closing control sits outside them. That distinction is drawn deliberately and correctly, which is rare enough to name.

**Theme-aware accent tokens with the reasoning recorded in place.** `--amber-text: #8F6018` in light with the measured 4.99:1 in a comment; `.prose a` moving its amber identity into the underline on light rather than failing at 2.4:1. The system doesn't just have a light mode, it has *decisions* about light mode, written where the next person will read them.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] A store-required privacy URL 404s onto an unbranded stock error page

**What:** `AppPageLayout` links every product to `/${slug}/privacy`, but `src/app/pica/privacy/page.tsx` does not exist — verified, `/pica/privacy` returns 404. There is no `src/app/not-found.tsx`, so the result is Next.js's stock white page: no nav, no theme, no brand, no exit. Compounding it, `/privacy`'s "App & Product Privacy" list is hardcoded and lists only four products — Pica is missing — because the list isn't mapped from `apps.ts`, contra CLAUDE.md's own stated convention.

**Why it matters:** This is the highest-volume persona's single job. Pica is the next product to ship, so the missing policy is a store-submission blocker, not just a broken link. A dead privacy link is the most damaging thing a tiny unknown studio can show a visitor whose entire reason for being there is deciding whether it's real.

**Fix:** Add `src/app/pica/privacy/page.tsx`. Map the `/privacy` list from `apps.ts` so a product can never silently fall out again. Add `src/app/not-found.tsx` with the dimmed `ConicOrb`, `Nav`, the wordmark, one honest sentence, and links to Home / Products / Privacy / Support.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The support form reports success it cannot verify

**What:** `SupportForm.handleSubmit` awaits `fetch("/", …)` and calls `setSubmitted(true)` on any resolved promise — verified, there is no `res.ok` check. `catch` fires only on network-level rejection. A Netlify 404/405 (form not registered after a build change — the classic Netlify Forms failure) renders "Thanks for reaching out!" for a message that was silently dropped.

**Why it matters:** Support is the site's only interactive flow and one of its two obligation surfaces. A user with a real bug believes they reported it, waits, hears nothing, and concludes the studio ignores people. The failure is invisible on both ends — Netlify Forms sends the user no email either.

**Fix:** `const res = await fetch(...); if (!res.ok) throw new Error(String(res.status));` so the existing, well-written `failed` alert actually fires. While in there: the success copy — "We've got your message and will reply by email as soon as we can" — is the peak-end moment of the entire site and currently its least reassuring sentence. Give it a concrete window and echo the selected product back.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Light-mode product titles fail WCAG AA, and Reviso renders two accents on one page

**What:** Measured independently by pixel sampling and by the injected detector, both agreeing: `/sixteen-to-one` `<h1>` Crema `#C4956A` on `#F1EFEF` = **2.33:1** at 40px; `/reviso` `<h1>` Cyan Deep `#06B6D4` on `#E7F3F6` = **2.14:1** at 36px. Both need 3:1. Dark mode is fine (6.71 and 9.73). Separately, `AppPageLayout` computes `accentColor = app.accentVar || app.accent` on line 23 but then uses raw `app.accent` for the hero border on line 40 — so `/reviso` draws its border in `#22D3EE` while its title uses `var(--reviso-accent)`. Two cyans, one page.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md holds WCAG AA "in *both* themes" as a commitment, not a cleanup, and these are the two most consequential product pages — the one shipped app and the developer product. The accent split breaks the "one accent per product" claim precisely where a design-literate developer is evaluating the studio.

**Fix:** Use the resolved `accentColor` for border, glow and dividers alike, not just the title. Add per-product light-mode title tones the way `--badge-*` already does (Sixteen to One → a deeper espresso-side tone; Reviso → `#0E7490`, already present in `globals.css`).

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] The accessibility commitment is 90% kept; three gaps sit in the remaining 10%

**What:** Three separate, individually small failures:

1. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is honored by the background and ignored by the foreground.** The `globals.css` block covers only `.fade-in` and `[data-reveal]` — verified. `animate-float`, `animate-scroll-hint` and `animate-pulse-dot` keep running; measured under `reduce`, `/` still runs 5 infinite animations and `/reviso` 1. `ConicOrb` gates itself correctly in JS, which makes the omission conspicuous: the decorative background respects the setting while the foreground does not.
2. **The full-viewport mobile menu leaves the page behind it focusable and announced.** At 390px with the menu open, focus continues past the menu's six rows into all five bento cell links and the footer's Privacy / Support / Terms — 8+ elements under an opaque overlay, with nothing `inert` or `aria-hidden`. The "disclosure, not modal" reasoning is correct about `aria-modal` and does not address occlusion.
3. **Bento cell accessible names concatenate.** Each announces as `"MobileSixteen to OneThe ratio, perfected.Available Now"` — all five product links open with the word "Mobile."

**Why it matters:** This is the population those settings exist for, and DESIGN.md's Do list already commits to all three. Reduced motion is a four-line CSS fix.

**Fix:** Extend the reduced-motion block to `.animate-float, .animate-scroll-hint, .animate-pulse-dot { animation: none; }`, giving the Coming Soon dot a static full-opacity state. Set `inert` on the page wrapper while the menu is open (keeps disclosure semantics, removes the occluded tab stops). `aria-hidden` the type chip and give each cell `<Link>` an explicit `aria-label`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] `/reviso` publishes pricing that PRODUCT.md marks undecided — and contradicts itself doing it

**What:** The page reads "Pay per org, not per seat. / A 10-person team and a 50-person team pay the same price. Usage-based pricing that scales with your work, not your headcount." Flat-per-org and usage-based are different pricing models, stated one sentence apart. PRODUCT.md sanctions the *structure* (BYOK, priced per org rather than per seat) as a settled bet and explicitly forbids publishing anything further as committed; "usage-based pricing" is a claim beyond it. The same page shows three empty phone-shaped frames labeled "SCREENSHOT" for a product that is a GitHub Action and has no screen, and carries a `SaaS` type chip on a BYOK action that runs on the customer's own runners — the opposite of SaaS, and a line that undercuts the studio's own "no servers" positioning on the homepage.

**Why it matters:** The developer persona is the one most likely to read carefully and the one whose product doesn't exist yet, so the page is pure credibility. A self-contradicting pricing paragraph and three empty placeholder boxes read as a page written before the product was thought through. And a convinced developer has nowhere to go — no `uses:` snippet, no repo, no `reviso.dev` link, no waitlist.

**Fix:** Cut "Usage-based pricing that scales with your work" and keep the per-org bet. Drop the placeholder carousel entirely — omission is more confident than three empty frames — and replace it with the smallest true artifact: a real `uses:` YAML block, or a sample inline review comment. Fix the type chip. Add one forward path at the end of the page.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Minor Observations

- `[data-reveal] { opacity: 0 }` gates the *entire* product grid on an IntersectionObserver. If JS fails or is blocked, the homepage is a hero and an empty page, with no `<noscript>` fallback. `.fade-in` compounds it: first paint is a blank gradient, and the scroll cue arrives ~1.2s later via staggered 0/200/400/600ms delays.
- `line-length` runs 85–96 characters on `/privacy` and `/support` — flagged by the detector, not sanctioned in DESIGN.md, and against the system's own stated priority of reading measure over container fill.
- `ThemeToggle` renders `<div className="w-11 h-11" />` until mounted, so the nav visibly pops on every cold load. Theme is also not synced across tabs (no `storage` listener).
- The inline theme script is `localStorage.getItem('vyttle-theme') || 'dark'`; `prefers-color-scheme` is never consulted, so a light-preferring visitor is served dark on first arrival. Defensible as a brand commitment — worth being deliberate rather than incidental about.
- `/terms` names only "Apple's Licensed Application End User License Agreement." Google Play is absent — the same iOS-only drift PRODUCT.md explicitly warns against at the studio level.
- `/sixteen-to-one` never says the app is free with an optional tip jar.
- App-page footer links declare `transition-colors duration-200` with no hover handler or hover class — dead code, and the only links on the site that don't respond to hover.
- In light mode the two store badges are the visually heaviest elements on `/sixteen-to-one` — two black slabs above product screenshots rendered at 180px thumbnails. The one shipped product's only real evidence is the smallest thing on its page.
- No `og:image`, no `metadataBase`, no canonical. A shared link to a privacy page renders as a bare text card.
- `/privacy` and `/terms` carry 8–9 `h2` sections with no in-page anchor navigation on mobile.
- The lightbox scrim is deliberately non-theme-aware (correct), but in light mode the fixed nav's own background reads through it as a brighter band at the top, and the close button sits inside that band — reading as a nav control rather than a dialog control.
- DESIGN.md's trailing "Known deltas" comment is stale on two of three points: `--midnight` is now `#0C0E1A`, and the input focus ring exists. Only delta 2 (`--badge-reviso` light `#0E7490` vs kit `#0891B2`) still stands.

## Questions to Consider

1. **The site's strongest asset — that it is built as the proof of its own privacy claim — appears nowhere a visitor will see it.** What if the homepage's second movement led with a single line under the promise: *"No analytics. No cookies. No consent banner. Nothing on this page called anyone but you."* Verified true, live. Would that one sentence do more for all three arrival paths than five cards ever will?

2. **The 3× hover jump is called "the personality of the entire system," and the majority of visitors are on phones where it cannot fire.** Is the signature interaction actually the hover — or the *transition between rest and presence*? What is its touch-native form: tint-on-press, a slow accent breathe on the shipped product alone, or accepting that mobile cells must carry 3× presence at rest?

3. **Four of five products say COMING SOON, and the site never names that as a position.** "Small by design" and "one shipped, four in progress" are the same fact told two ways, and they sit a hundred pixels apart without ever being connected. What changes if the grid is explicitly framed — a status line, an eyebrow, an ordering that reads as a roadmap rather than a portfolio with gaps?

4. **Both obligation pages are excellent prose and mediocre documents.** They never name Vyttle LLC or Florida, they carry stale dates, and mobile navigation offers no path to them at all. If the front door really is `/privacy`, why is it reachable only from a 15px-tall link at the bottom of the footer — and why does the door never say who lives here?

5. **Light mode is where the observatory becomes a gradient.** The Perceptual Parity Rule (light needs ~2× alpha to register) and the Look-Through Rule (never notice the background before the words) are in genuine conflict, and light mode currently resolves it toward parity. Which rule is actually load-bearing for the brand — and if it's Look-Through, what is the light-mode atmosphere that registers without becoming the subject?
